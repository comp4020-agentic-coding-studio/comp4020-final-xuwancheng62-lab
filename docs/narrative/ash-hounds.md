# The Ash Hounds

Labels are explained in [README.md](README.md#status-labels).

**[Canon]** (the approved direction):
- A murderous raider faction, the **Ash Hounds**, led by **Wade Mercer**.
- They ambush travellers and supply convoys, kill people, steal food, clean
  water, medicine and equipment, raid shelters, prey on isolated survivors,
  and rule by threats and violence. These are deliberate choices.
- They are villains. Understanding them makes them believable; it never
  excuses them. They are not a misunderstood rationing authority and are not
  secretly working for the common good.
- Wade was a freight dispatcher before the war who knew the region's
  warehouses and transport, and chose predation over cooperation.
- Their base is in or around an old highway checkpoint.
- "Ash Hounds" is a name only. No link to the scavenger beasts, which are
  dogs.

Everything else on this page is **[Proposed]**.

Violence is shown through consequences: graves, a toolbag left behind, a
chained valve, a tag on a familiar door, survivors' accounts. No gore for its
own sake, and nothing here is real-world tactics.

## The faction

**What holds them together**: water, food and a dry, warm building through
winter; complicity, since Wade makes sure everyone has blood on their hands
("nobody walks away to tell it"); fear, since leaving makes you prey; Wade's
competence, since his runs work and people eat.

**Size and reach**: about 22: 14 who ride on raids, plus lookouts, cooks and
a quartermaster; 3–4 forced workers, including a cartridge re-packer taken
from Kell Bridge. Their ground is the highway corridor between Calder and
Kell Bridge, the Ridge Road, and Calder's east side. They stay away from
Northfield (walled, guarded), the depot underpass (the dogs) and the hot
ground west toward Port Sallow.

**The base, "the Weighbridge"**: the concrete weigh station and police post
on the highway at the Mulga Creek bridge. Barriers of jack-knifed trailers;
two shipping containers as stores and cells; the scale-house office, where
Wade works.

**How they live between raids**: tribute from tagged shelters on a schedule
(cartridges, tins, scrap, seed); tolls on walkers and traders; Kell Bridge's
water held hostage by the chained valve; their own water from stolen
cartridges re-packed by captives. They grow almost nothing. One wood-gas ute;
otherwise bikes and a horse cart.

**How they look**: grey coats, mostly old hi-vis dyed grey; respirators;
yellow freight consignment tags (Wade's habit) on taken goods and wired to
shelters they've "assessed"; "AH" and an arrow stencilled on the road toward
the next target.

**How survivors know they're near**: a tag on a door; a stencil on the
bitumen; smoke from burnt tyres on the ridge; a cache found empty; a walker
who doesn't arrive.

**What they do to people**
1. Kill on the road: ambush walkers, traders and convoys, and kill witnesses
   on purpose (the Ridge Road drivers; Nell Ashby).
2. Murdered Dev Pillai at the intake.
3. Hold Kell Bridge's water hostage with the chained valve.
4. Extort shelters: tagged shelters pay tribute on a schedule; those that
   refuse are raided; isolated ones are hit hardest.
5. Take people: forced labour (the Kell Bridge re-packer), forced recruits
   (Jace Tully), and the ledger wants Mags "whole".
6. Rule by fear: tolls, road stencils, a regular schedule. Holdouts learn to
   stay home on Thursdays and never travel alone.

They also sharpen the game's own tension: anyone who leaves home to help
someone else leaves their own shelter unguarded.

**Why they're dangerous**: ambush, not fair fights; isolated targets; dead
witnesses; Wade knows where pre-war stock sits and how routes are timed; a
few guns with little ammunition, so they rely on surprise and intimidation.

**Limits and weaknesses**: no clean water of their own; almost no food of
their own; predictable (Thursday runs); everything written down (the ledger);
captives and unwilling recruits; the dogs; winter; only about fourteen who
ride.

**Why nobody has beaten them**: holdouts are scattered and don't share
information; leaving home to fight leaves your own shelter open; tribute costs
less than fighting, for now; Kell Bridge is small and defensive; Northfield
looks inward.

**What could change that**: shared information (their schedule, the ledger);
an open valve; a witness; defections; shelters keeping watch for each other.

## Wade Mercer

About 50. Before the war: dispatch lead at the FreshWay Regional distribution
centre in Ender. On the Ninth he processed the ESD-31 trucks himself, and
noticed FreshWay Calder sent 11 of 22 (`dispatch-id`).

- **Manner**: calm and courteous; freight language: "runs", "consignments",
  "on time", "off the manifest". Never shouts.
- **Wants now**: the corridor's water and trade under his control before
  winter, and the old woman who can fix any Unit.
- **Loyalty and failure**: loyalty earns first pick of the loot, a place
  inside the scale house, double water. Failure means gate duty in the cold.
  The worst is being "left off the manifest": no water.
- **Why they obey**: competence, food, fear, shared guilt.
- **His culpability**: at the Ridge Road in Y+1 he ordered three drivers
  killed after they had surrendered, so nobody could report the route. In
  Y+4 he had Dev Pillai killed for refusing to hand over the valve chamber.
  He knows what his choices cost and keeps making them. No tragic backstory
  stands in for that.
- **Weakness**: he needs order and to keep face. His runs are predictable,
  his ledger is complete, and open defiance makes him overreact, as when Ruth
  refused and he chained the valve, which ties his people to a guard post.
- **How the player meets him before meeting him**: Toby's chalk ("Mercer's
  lot"); yellow tags; a grave marker naming no one; the ledger signed W.M.;
  his staff ID; then, last, the man.
- **Not responsible for**: the war, the Ninth, Helen's death, or most of the
  valley's losses.

## Supporting characters

Kept to people with a distinct job in the story.

**Lena Voss** (40s, quartermaster). A pharmacy assistant before the war.
Keeps the ledger and the medicine. Keeps her own count of the dead, in the
back of the ledger; that doesn't excuse her. Wants to leave west with the
medicine. Story role: a possible deal, her copy of the ledger for safe
passage, with a real cost.

**Jace Tully** (19). Made to join when his family's shelter was raided in Y+2;
has hurt people since. The ledger: "Tully hesitated again. Last warning."
Story role: the crack. A possible informant, and an uncomfortable choice: help
him leave, use him, or hand him to Kell Bridge.

**Sergeant Colin Bell** (historical, gone in Y+1). Ran the checkpoint levy and
objected to killing. His levy receipts show how the faction began
(`levy-receipt`).

## In the main story

The Ash Hounds appear in two major threads only: the Ridge Road convoy
(Helen's) and the intake ambush (Dev and Toby). Ruth meets their predecessors
at the levy. Other outcomes in the valley come from war, illness, migration
and ordinary choices. How the player finds and responds to them is in
[main-story.md](main-story.md); the records are in [fragments.md](fragments.md).

**Not decided**: any Ash Hound encounter mechanic. The scavenger beast stays
the first and only implemented fight. Any future raider encounter would use
the existing equipment-loss rules, and any story raid on a player's shelter
the existing raid-loss limits; no shelter is ever destroyed.

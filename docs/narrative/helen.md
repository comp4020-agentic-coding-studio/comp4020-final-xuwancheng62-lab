## Who she is

**[Proposed]** Helen believed a fair list could stand in for a fair world.
Since the Civil Resilience Scheme began (Y-6) she had drawn up Calder's
shelter allocations. Owners got a subsidised Unit; renters, flats and the
caravan park got a numbered place at a Community Shelter Point. She thought
that was fair because everyone was *on a list*. She gave the siren talk at
every school and ended it the same way every time: "Everyone's on a list."

She and Ruth are alike, which is why they fight. Both run queues. Both say
"same for everyone". Each, once, made an exception for someone they loved,
and neither has said so to the other.

**Wanted**:
- before the war, an orderly evacuation, with nobody left off;
- on the Ninth, her mother safe;
- after it, to make Northfield's lists fair enough to make up for 12:40;
- at the end, to tell her mother the truth.

**Contradiction**: she demands information discipline, and broke it for her
mother. She wouldn't answer her mother's notes for two years, then spent her
last strength writing one.

## Choices and who paid

1. **The 08:00 bulletin** [Proposed]. It was true at 08:00. She never
   corrected it, because the 13:45 bulletin said "shelter now" and she
   thought that covered it. Households on the mains drank tap water after
   the dust arrived. The cost is diffuse and nobody can be named; she knows
   it anyway.
2. **12:40** [Proposed]. She held the forecast for an hour and five minutes
   so a crowd wouldn't wait on the open highway for a bus. That was a
   procedural reason, and partly a good one. In the same minute she rang
   her mother.
   - **Who paid**: the east-side households waiting for Bus 3, who had 1h45
     instead of 2h50 to get indoors.
   - **Who gained**: CS-4, which sealed by two.
   - She told herself why for three years and "it still isn't true" (her
     letter).
3. **Silence** [Proposed]. She received her mother's notes and didn't
   answer. Shame over 12:40, the pallets argument, pride. Ruth paid for
   this, with five years of not knowing.
4. **The convoy** [Proposed]. She spent a walled town's last fuel on another
   town's water and medicine, against a vote, partly because her mother was
   there.
   - **Who paid**: the three drivers, and Northfield, which has no fuel
     reserve since.
   - **What she gained**: nothing arrived.
   - It is also the act the Ash Hounds' name comes from: victims started
     calling them that after the Ridge Road ([ash-hounds.md](ash-hounds.md#history)).
5. **The letter** [Proposed]. The one time she tells the whole truth. It
   never arrives.

She is not responsible for the war, the plume, the checkpoint closing, or
the Ash Hounds' killings. The comic shows her choices and their costs
without a verdict.

## Comic: "Twelve Forty"

14 panels on 8 pages, in the format of [toby-comic-script.md](toby-comic-script.md).
Text kinds: **N** narration, **S** speech (with speaker), **W** aside. Words
are HTML over the art. Each card's object is marked **Object**.

**The arc**: a woman who trusts lists breaks one rule for her mother, spends
three years making other lists fair to make up for it, breaks a bigger rule
for her mother's town, pays for both, and tells the truth too late.

**What it doesn't do**:
- **It doesn't quote the letter's text.** Opening it is the player's
  choice, and Helen's own words stay in the record.
- **It doesn't show the ambush**, only the aftermath.
- **The Ash Hounds and Wade aren't named or shown.**
- **It doesn't judge her.**

It does tell the writer's truth plainly, including her death, because the
set only completes with the letter, at the Weighbridge.

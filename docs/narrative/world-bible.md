## Premise

**[Canon]** Five years ago a short nuclear war hit military and industrial
targets across the region. Calder Valley, a fictional inland valley in
south-eastern Australia, wasn't struck. It lay downwind of two strikes, and
its supply, power and transport collapsed with everyone else's. People
survived where they had shelter, water they could treat, and food that had
stayed sealed. The players live in former household **Resilience Units**,
inherited or claimed after the people before them left or died. The player
did nothing wrong to get theirs.

The experience: *I leave a shelter I care about, enter a world damaged by
nuclear war, and discover how ordinary people tried to survive, and what
their choices left for people like me.*

## Before the war

**[Canon]**
- After an earlier international crisis, the federal **Civil Resilience
  Scheme** (from Y-6) subsidised household Resilience Units. The subsidy
  needed a co-payment and a house you owned with a suitable block. Renters,
  flats and the caravan park got **Community Shelter Points** instead (CS-1
  to CS-6, room for about a quarter of the town). CS-4 was FreshWay's
  basement car park.
- Two years of standoff made war background noise: school siren drills, then
  purchase limits at FreshWay in the weeks before.

**[Proposed]** Ordinary life: school runs, footy, the Loop carts chiming down
the streets, Saturday shifts at FreshWay, Unit inspections as a running joke
that Mags made a living out of.

## The Loop

**[Canon]** The council's resource programme: waste-collection carts, a
compost depot under the bypass, and servicing for Units. It caused nothing.
Its depot is the Creature Nest; its carts and parts trade are how shelters
keep running. Toby's worksheet (`our-loop`) is the player's view of it.

**[Proposed]** A few solar-topped carts still wake on bright days, run part
of a route and play the chime.

### The bypass

**[Canon, 2026-10-10]** in outline; the details below are **[Proposed]**.

Calder's first waterworks (1890s) emptied its silt with a **scour line**: a
brick culvert from the old intake that runs round the outside of the valve
chamber and rejoins the old works main below it. When the main was
rebuilt, the scour line was blanked off and left off the later drawings. Dev
found it in the council's first-works plans and used it to keep water moving
on the autumns he cleared the intake. It is the only way water reaches the
main without passing the main valve.

- **Where**: its gate, a cast-iron sluice in a small brick chamber, is deep
  in the gallery beyond the valve chamber. The way to it is the scour
  drain, the side pipe Dev sent Toby down on 18 May; it runs on toward the
  depot culvert, which is how Toby got out. The gate is within earshot of
  the guard the Ash Hounds keep at the chained valve.
- **Why it isn't an easy fix**:
  - **It's half-silted.** The reservoir silt is where the fallout settled.
    Open the gate quickly or too far and the first flush carries that silt
    down the main to Kell Bridge for weeks. Nothing at the tap fixes that:
    not boiling, not an ordinary filter.
  - **It opens a quarter turn at a time, over days.** Between turns the
    intake screens have to be cleared and the first water run through
    freshly re-packed cartridges downstream. Dev's re-pack card
    (`dev-repack-card` [Proposed]) holds the steps.
  - **It takes at least two people who know the procedure.** Toby knows the
    way, because he crawled past the gate, but only part of the procedure.
    Mags knows re-packing. Kell Bridge's re-packers would have to be ready
    for the first water.
  - **It's next to the guard.** Days of quiet work, a few metres from the
    people who killed Dev.
- **What it would change**: Kell Bridge would have water the Ash Hounds
  don't control, and the chained valve would stop being worth guarding. In
  the main story this is the "Reopen the valve" choice, now "open the
  bypass" ([main-story.md](main-story.md#choices)). It's also what Toby
  means in his letter: "I'm staying till the valve's open."

**Why help never came back**: no fuel; the cities took what capacity was
left; the state's coordination broke into towns that look after their own
water; a few hundred people with nothing to trade aren't worth a convoy.

**Why working shelters sit beside dangerous ruins**: Units were built
hardened and off-grid, and people patch them constantly. Everything else was
left to rot.

## Radiation (story rules only)

**[Canon]** Radiation is part of the world, not a game system. No meter,
exposure stat, protective gear stat or treatment mechanic exists or is
approved. Nothing here is real-world safety advice.

1. **Two different dangers.** Exposure is being near contaminated material.
   Contamination is carrying it: on dust, clothes, boots, objects, water,
   food. Shelters guard mostly against the second.
2. **Time.** The worst passed in weeks; CS-4 "sat tight two weeks". Five
   years on, most open ground can be crossed and searched. Danger gathers
   where dust and water collect: reservoir silt, drains, gutters, sumps,
   spent cartridges, the depot's heaps, unsealed food.
3. **Underground helps; it isn't immunity.** Earth and concrete shield a Unit.
   Its weak points are what gets carried in: the hatch's wipe-down room (the
   "dust room"), the air intake filter, and the water. A cracked seal or a
   saturated cartridge lets the outside in.
4. **Water.** Boiling, or an ordinary filter, does **not** remove it. A Unit
   purifier settles, prefilters, binds contaminants in a **sorbent stack** of
   RC-40 cartridges (fictional), then uses UV for germs. Cartridges saturate,
   become hot waste in the cartridge pit, and must be re-packed. Kell Bridge
   and Mags can re-pack them. Reservoir water is carried home and treated,
   never drunk at the pool.
5. **Food.** Sealed pre-war food is clean inside, which is why the
   supermarket matters. Surface-grown crops carry contamination. Unit
   planters work with bagged pre-war mix, treated water and lamps, which is
   why they need power.
6. **Measurement.** Meters are rare and batteries rarer. Records say "above
   the line" or "under the line", never numbers, and people act "on trust".

**[Proposed]** Possible later gameplay, none approved: exposure that a trip
adds to; cartridge wear as purifier upkeep; a dust-room step on return;
seasonal hot places; protective gear as a gear slot; a meter item that
shows hot spots.

**Water notice audit.** No record says boiling handles contamination. If a
pre-war "boil water" notice is ever used, it must be dated before the war and
be about germs.

## Creatures

**[Canon]**
- Three kinds of threat: ordinary feral animals; fictionally mutated animals;
  pre-war machines.
- The mutation premise is openly fictional: populations that fed for
  **generations** on hot places become "fallout-born": larger, patchy hide,
  heavy jaw and brow, fiercely territorial. Nothing changes within one
  animal's life.
- **The scavenger beast** is a feral dog pack descended from the depot dogs,
  some fallout-born. They den and hoard in the underpass and answer the old
  cart chime. One animal meets you per trip. No record says any one animal is
  one of Toby's dogs. It is an animal surviving, not a monster.
- Art should move toward a recognisable dog. The current
  `static/img/shelter/beast.webp` (from the "hyena crossed with a giant rat"
  prompt in `scripts/generate-shelter-art.ts`) doesn't set lore.

**[Proposed]** The pack is in its second to fourth generation. The one you
meet is a sentry guarding the den and stores, worse when there are pups or
food is short. The Ash Hounds avoid the underpass because of the dogs.

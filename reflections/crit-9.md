# Crit 9 reflection

## What was the breakthrough that moved the work forward?

The breakthrough was connecting the game's systems so that each activity gives
another one a purpose. Exploration produces clues, clues reconstruct someone's
story, and completing that story can lead to recruiting them. I then decided
that recruited NPCs should be full characters who can equip items, develop and
explore, rather than only provide passive bonuses.

This direction also shaped multiplayer expeditions. Players contribute one
character each, and friends can dispatch a designated character while its owner
is offline. Cooperation therefore involves preparation and trust, not just
being online at the same time.

Friends tried basic raiding and leaving messages. This was a limited playtest:
it did not cover the later expedition systems. Those changes are now merged
into local main, but I need to distinguish that development milestone from
evidence about how well they play.

## What did this work change about who I want to be as a software developer?

I want to become better at expressing an intended experience and evaluating
the result. With an agent, detailed instructions can look precise while still
producing the wrong outcome. When I allowed comic speech bubbles outside the
image, they ended up far from the characters. A simpler instruction about
proximity and keeping faces visible communicated my intention better.

I also learned to challenge proposals that did not match my goals, such as
treating NPCs only as bonuses or restricting Toby's participation in parties.
My responsibility is to make those design choices and check their consequences.
More implemented features are useful only if they form an understandable and
worthwhile experience.

# Crit 8 reflection

The breakthrough for me was realising that a working system was not yet the game I wanted to make. Holdout already had accounts, resources, journeys, and persistent state, but looking at the deployed version, I felt I was reading a set of tables rather than inhabiting a shelter. That gap made me reconsider what “good” meant.

I began with the idea that leaving home should involve a trade-off: you need supplies, but your shelter becomes unguarded. Through reviewing the interface and discussing the design, I realised that this only matters if the player first cares about the place they are leaving. Moving characters and visible construction could help create that attachment. Exploration could also offer more than supplies: discovering what happened to the world could give players another reason to leave. Helping, robbing, and negotiating with other shelters could then turn survival into a series of social decisions. These are directions to test, not features I can already claim to have delivered.

This changed how I think about directing an agent. I need to explain the experience a feature should create, then judge whether the implementation supports it. A completed feature list cannot make that judgement for me.

I want to become a developer who can connect implementation decisions to a clear purpose, while remaining willing to revise that purpose through use and feedback. My next challenge is restraint: building a small, convincing shelter interaction before expanding into all the systems I can imagine.
